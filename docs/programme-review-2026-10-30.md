# Programme review — 30 October 2026

## Aim and interpretation

Build or retain muscle in the chest, shoulders, arms, back and abdomen while reducing body fat gradually. The assessment below is a programming judgement informed by the cited research. These studies do not test this exact seven-tab routine, establish an individual's ideal set count, validate the displayed sample weights, or guarantee a physique or body-fat outcome in 52 weeks.

The latest request makes both weekend sessions optional. The original four core days and exercise preferences remain the foundation: Tuesday retains squat, standing overhead press and conventional deadlift; Monday retains Captain's Chair Knee Raise. No floor abs or awkward new cable work is introduced.

## Review of the core week

| Day | Exercise coverage | Assessment and practical limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Bench, incline dumbbell press, supported row, cable fly, lateral raise, pushdown, Captain's Chair | Good chest priority with lateral shoulder, triceps and supported abdominal work. The supported row supplies a second back exposure. Pressing also contributes triceps/front-shoulder work; more arm sets are not automatically needed. |
| Tuesday | Squat, standing overhead press, conventional deadlift, leg curl, calf raise | Maintains strength practice and lower-body balance. Squat plus deadlift is demanding: alternate emphasis, use progressive warm-ups, and avoid maximal efforts on both. Monday pressing can affect overhead-press performance; adjust load accordingly. |
| Wednesday | Pull-ups/assisted pull-ups, supported row, neutral pulldown, reverse pec deck, EZ curl, hammer curl, hanging raise | Appropriate vertical/horizontal pulling and rear-shoulder/arm work for an athletic upper body. Supported rowing limits additional unsupported trunk work after Tuesday. Pull-ups and pulldowns overlap; reduce one if session quality or recovery deteriorates. |
| Thursday | Leg press, split squat, Romanian deadlift, leg curl, incline press machine, lateral raise, overhead triceps extension, calf raise | Adds a second leg/chest exposure using practical equipment. The Romanian deadlift is the main fatigue overlap with Tuesday; reduce or skip it when not recovered. It is the longest core session, so adding sets should not be the default response to a plateau. |
| Friday — optional | Lateral raise, preacher curl, pushdown, rear-delt fly, incline walking | Reasonable extra shoulder/arm work, provided the core sessions are recovering well. It carries no exercise required to complete the core plan. |

Four consecutive core days are a scheduling compromise, rather than a claim that consecutive training is optimal. Monitor performance across the week. The supported exercises and fatigue adjustments are practical choices for this existing schedule.

## Weekly direct sets, before optional work

| Muscle group | Sets | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Chest | 11 | Main physique priority; presses and fly cover it across two days. |
| Back | 10 | Vertical pulls and supported rows across two days. |
| Shoulders | 11 | Spread across different heads; not 11 sets for every head. Presses also contribute. |
| Biceps | 6 | Direct curls plus indirect pulling work. |
| Triceps | 6 | Direct work plus indirect pressing. |
| Quads | 8 | Squat, leg press and split squat. |
| Hamstrings | 6 | Leg curls and Romanian deadlift, plus additional conventional-deadlift stimulus. |
| Calves | 5 | Two core exposures; progress these before adding volume. |
| Abs | 6 | Supported/hanging raises; extra abs do not replace the need for overall fat loss. |

These are reasonable starting volumes, not equally optimal prescriptions for every muscle. The 2026 ACSM guidance gives approximately 10 weekly sets per muscle as a useful hypertrophy benchmark. That is not a mandatory minimum for growth, and arm work from compound lifts matters. [ACSM guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)

A newer dose-response review found that more volume can support additional gains, but with diminishing returns; indirect sets also contribute. This supports adjusting volume to response rather than automatically adding hard sessions. [Pelland et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/)

## Distinct optional weekends

### Saturday: supported lower-body, calves and abs

| Exercise | Starting guide |
| --- | --- |
| Leg extension machine | 2 × 12–15 |
| Seated leg curl, lighter than core days | 2 × 12–15 |
| Seated calf raise | 2 × 12–20 |
| Seated abdominal crunch machine | 2 × 10–15 |
| Stationary bike | 15–20 minutes at an easy/moderate conversational pace |

Use this only when Thursday's leg work has recovered. Select fewer movements or rest if needed. The additional sets are optional; the core plan does not depend on them. Machine loads are examples that must be calibrated to the actual machine and the user's performance.

### Sunday: conditioning and standing mobility

| Exercise | Starting guide |
| --- | --- |
| Elliptical trainer, or an easy walk | 20–30 minutes at an easy conversational pace |
| Standing wall slide | 2 × 8–12 comfortable reps |
| Supported standing hip circles | 1 × 8–10 each direction on each side |

This avoids adding hard chest/shoulder/arm work immediately before Monday. Mobility is comfortable movement practice, not additional hypertrophy volume or a treatment claim. No floor exercise is needed. Rest is an equally valid Sunday choice; seven hard gym days are not the target.

Combined aerobic and resistance training generally supported whole-muscle hypertrophy and maximal strength in a 43-study review; explosive strength was more sensitive, particularly with same-session training. Easy weekend conditioning is a practical application, not proof that any cardio dose is harmless. [Schumann et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/)

For general health, WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week, including activity outside the gym. These short optional blocks alone do not necessarily reach that total; build overall activity gradually. This is a health benchmark, not an exact fat-loss prescription. [WHO](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/physical-activity)

## Nutrition, progression and the 52-week roadmap

- Maintaining muscle while losing fat requires more than extra training. Keep the existing modest calorie deficit as an initial estimate and adjust using several weeks of weight/waist trends, strength and recovery. A meta-analysis found that larger sustained energy deficits impair lean-mass gains; its average estimate is not an individual calorie threshold. [Murphy & Koehler, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34623696/)
- The existing 2.05 g/kg protein setting is a reasonable starting choice within this programme, rather than a unique optimum. Evidence supports sufficient protein alongside resistance training; additional whey is not compulsory and does not guarantee additional growth. [Morton et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)
- Retain controlled rep progression and small practical load increases. Training experience alone does not establish a safe working weight; actual completed sets guide the suggestions.
- Treat the four phases as a review framework. Move to maintenance or a surplus based on actual leanness, recovery and performance, rather than automatically bulking because a numbered week has arrived.
- Do not increase every exercise's sets while dieting. Reduce optional work first if core performance, sleep, soreness or enthusiasm deteriorate.
